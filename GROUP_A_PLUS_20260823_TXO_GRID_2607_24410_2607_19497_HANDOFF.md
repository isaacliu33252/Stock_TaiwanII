## Part 1: TXO Put Overlay — Strike-Depth x Premium-Budget Grid Search

### Background

Direct continuation of the explicitly flagged next step from
`2607_00883_txo_put_overlay_deeper_otm_revalidation` (documented in the
prior handoff): 85%/80%-of-spot strike depths restore real crisis-specific
protection (esp. the 2025 tariff shock) but leave full-window Sharpe at
noise level. The open question left behind was whether some combination of
strike depth and premium budget clears a genuine full-window Sharpe bar.

### Method

New script: `scripts/evaluate/build_txo_put_overlay_depth_budget_grid_2607_00883.py`.
7x5 = 35-cell grid: OTM depth in {90%, 87.5%, 85%, 82.5%, 80%, 77.5%, 75%}
of spot x annual premium budget in {1.0%, 1.5%, 2.0%, 2.5%, 3.0%} of NAV,
all under the same realistic integer-contract sizing established in the
affordable-subset work.

### Result

Only 2/35 cells (5.7%) meet both `d_sharpe > 0.02` and `d_mdd` improvement
simultaneously:

| depth | budget | d_sharpe | d_mdd |
|---|---|---|---|
| 77.5% | 1.0% | +0.041 | +0.06pp |
| 77.5% | 1.5% | +0.024 | +0.03pp |

Both improvements are small and comparable in magnitude to the noise floor
already established in the deeper-OTM revalidation (±0.015). **Critically,
the grid is not smooth or monotonic around these "winning" cells** — e.g.
85% depth at 1.5% budget shows d_sharpe=+0.0036, while the *adjacent* 87.5%
depth at the same 1.5% budget shows d_sharpe=-0.015. A genuine structural
relationship between strike depth/budget and overlay quality would produce
a smooth transition between neighboring cells; this sign-flipping, jagged
pattern is the signature of multiple-comparisons noise, not a real sweet
spot — directly the risk flagged by `feedback_overfitting_fixed_window_tuning`
(fixed-window multi-round parameter tuning is an overfitting risk).

### Conclusion

`closed_negative`. The grid search did not find a genuine full-window-
Sharpe-positive combination. The nominal "best" cells (77.5% depth, low
budget) are explicitly **not** adopted or reported as a discovery —
treating them as one would be exactly the overfitting mistake this rule
exists to prevent. This closes the flagged open question from the deeper-
OTM revalidation with a negative answer, reinforcing (not overturning)
that entry's `shadow_track` / do-not-promote status.

## Part 2: arXiv:2607.24410 — CD-DFM Characteristic-Driven Covariance

### Paper

Alouadi & Lehalle, "The Fundamental Structure of Risk: From
Characteristics to Covariance." Proposes CD-DFM: a latent factor model
that builds a cross-sectional covariance representation of a large equity
universe (S&P 500, N=239) directly from firm **fundamentals** (size,
book-to-market, leverage, ROE, capex/revenue, geographic/commodity revenue
mix, GICS sector, plus minor news signals) — explicitly excluding price/
return-derived features (momentum, realized vol, beta) — trained end-to-
end on a Stein covariance loss plus factor-reconstruction term, predicting
the forward N x N return covariance matrix for GMV/ERC portfolio
construction. Claimed advantages: characteristic-driven latent space
(zero-shot embedding for unseen stocks), interpretable regime-stable
sector factors, and covariance calibration competitive with or better than
PCA/Sparse PCA/IPCA/GKX/Ledoit-Wolf/nonlinear shrinkage despite using much
lower-frequency information than return-based estimators.

### Applicability assessment (desk review only — no backtest run)

Same premise-level disqualification standard already used this session for
`2608_20020_reconfiguration_premium_correlation_rotation`, another paper
requiring a large individual-stock cross-section Group A+ does not have.
Two independent, each-sufficient reasons:

1. **The statistical problem CD-DFM solves does not exist for Group A+.**
   The paper's own motivation: a 239-stock universe with ~700 daily return
   observations has N approaching/exceeding T, making the sample
   covariance matrix singular/unstable — exactly what the low-rank-plus-
   diagonal factor structure fixes. Group A+'s actual traded universe is a
   handful of ETFs (0050, 00631L, 00632R, plus a small defensive basket —
   at most ~10-15 instruments across all research this session), each with
   return histories running to thousands of trading days. N is far smaller
   than T; the plain sample covariance matrix is already stable and
   invertible.

2. **The required input features do not naturally exist for Group A+'s
   instruments.** CD-DFM's encoder consumes individual-firm fundamentals.
   Group A+ trades index/leveraged/inverse ETF wrappers, not individual
   companies — there is no natural per-instrument fundamental-
   characteristic vector to feed it. A weighted aggregate of 0050's
   underlying constituents' fundamentals is technically constructible but
   would not solve any problem Group A+ actually has: the risk-management
   question Group A+ needs answered is the covariance structure among its
   handful of ETF-level positions, not a reconstruction of a 239-stock
   cross-sectional covariance.

The paper's own proposed extension (characteristic-to-covariance map as a
what-if stress-testing tool, in its Limitations section) inherits the same
large-individual-stock-cross-section premise and does not change the
verdict.

### Conclusion

`closed_negative`, decided at desk review — the structural mismatch
(universe size, feature type) is decisive on its own, so no
reimplementation was attempted.

## Part 3: arXiv:2607.19497 — Trend-Following Closed-Form ACF/Sharpe Framework

### Paper and proposed application

Sepp & Lucic, "The Science and Practice of Trend-Following Systems."
Derives closed-form expected return, Sharpe ratio (gross and net of
costs), cost-optimal filter span, and skewness for EWMA-filter trend-
following (TF) systems, as explicit functions of the autocorrelation
function and drift of volatility-normalized returns z_t = r_t / sigma_{t-1}.
Validated on 84 liquid futures contracts (pooled predicted-vs-realized
Sharpe correlation 0.99, regression slope 0.96). Classifies TF systems as
European (continuous EWMA-filtered signal), American (binary breakout/
trailing-stop, "turtle trader" style), and TSMOM (sign-of-momentum); the
paper itself validates its European closed-form against realized American/
TSMOM performance too.

User's proposed application: Group A+'s golden1/00631L regime switch is
structurally a form of trend-following (closest to the paper's own
"American TF" classification), and the paper's closed-form autocorrelation-
to-Sharpe framework could give a complete theoretical basis for when to
hold vs. exit 00631L.

### Method — tested directly, not desk-reviewed

Replicated the paper's own diagnostic and in-sample verification
methodology (Section 5 Corollary 5.5, Section 7.3) on 0050.TW and
00631L.TW. New script:
`scripts/evaluate/build_trend_following_acf_sharpe_2607_19497.py`.
Computed EWMA volatility (paper's own 33-day span, eq. 2.13) and
volatility-normalized returns z_t; estimated the sample ACF of z_t;
applied the closed-form Sharpe ratio (eq. 5.12) across filter spans
{5, 10, 21, 63, 125, 250, 500} days; built an **actual** single-filter
European TF system per the paper's own Definition 4.1 (continuous EWMA-
filtered, variance-preserving) and backtested its realized Sharpe to
verify the closed form transfers to Taiwan-ETF data — matching the
paper's own verification approach rather than only computing the
theoretical prediction.

**Two bugs caught and fixed before finalizing:**
1. 0050.TW has 183 exact-zero-return days (mostly thin 2009-2013 trading),
   which decay EWMA variance toward zero during runs, causing the next
   real return's z_t to blow up (near-zero denominator) — fixed with a
   variance floor.
2. First backtest pass omitted the paper's own sigma_target/sqrt(a)
   prefactor from eq. (4.3), producing absurd annualized return/vol
   figures (~2000%). Did not affect the Sharpe ratio itself
   (scale-invariant), but was fixed before drawing conclusions for a
   legible report.

### Result

- Sample ACF of z_t is essentially negligible on both instruments: mean
  ACF over lags 1-20 is +0.0048 (0050) and +0.0050 (00631L).
- 0050's lag-1 ACF (+0.035) sits at the low end of the paper's own
  observed range across its 84 liquid futures contracts (0.01-0.04);
  00631L's lag-1 ACF (+0.003) is an order of magnitude smaller —
  essentially indistinguishable from zero.
- Applying the paper's own break-even-cost formula (eq. 6.13) to 0050's
  lag-1 autocorrelation gives a break-even cost of only ~28bp — below
  realistic transaction costs for a systematic single-ETF strategy. The
  paper's *own* theory says this magnitude of short-memory
  predictability is not exploitable net of costs.
- **Key finding**: at every span tested, on both instruments, the
  European TF system's predicted *and* realized Sharpe (0050: 0.33-0.38;
  00631L: 0.29-0.68) sit well below the instrument's own buy-and-hold
  Sharpe (mu_z_an: 0050 = 0.629, 00631L = 0.978) — including at the
  longest 500-day span. Per the paper's own Section 6.1 / Remark C.3, a
  TF system converges toward buy-and-hold (at a deterministic discount)
  only in the long-span limit when driven by drift rather than genuine
  autocorrelation — exactly what's observed here: 0050/00631L's Sharpe is
  almost entirely attributable to their own strong long-term drift, not
  exploitable serial dependence. A trend-following overlay approximates
  buy-and-hold worse than buy-and-hold itself, at every span tested.
- Predicted and realized Sharpe tracked consistently across all spans and
  both tickers (~0.03-0.04 gap), confirming the closed-form methodology
  itself transferred correctly to Taiwan-ETF data — this is a negative
  finding about the underlying autocorrelation structure, not a
  methodology failure.

### Conclusion

`closed_negative`. Not because the golden1/00631L switch is wrongly
classified as trend-following (the American-TF structural analogy is
reasonable, and consistent with the paper's own taxonomy), but because
0050/00631L simply lack the autocorrelation structure (near-zero, an
order of magnitude below the paper's own liquid-futures universe) that
would make this closed-form span-selection framework add value over
buy-and-hold.

**Connection to earlier session finding**: this is a second, independent,
theoretically-grounded confirmation of the same underlying fact already
found via the 2606.23492 CHMM regime-detector test earlier this session —
pure price-autocorrelation-based or volatility-regime-based signals don't
carry much edge on these two Taiwan ETFs. Whatever edge Group A+'s switch
rule has must come from its richer feature set (MA-gap trend distance,
drawdown depth, chip/derivatives positioning), not from genuine price-
momentum/trend-persistence the way CTA trend-following systems monetize
trend on liquid futures.
